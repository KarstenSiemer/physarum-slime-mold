- The words: **the rules** are the rules of the growth front (the setting `territory`). **Fresh ground** is ground

  memory that fresh ground starts with, 0.2: it is 20 times the level 0.01 below which no agent is born.

## Summary

**1. The flop is a bug of the auto levels, not of the model.** The white level of the picture is the trail that 1 %
of all cells of the grid exceed (`src/engine/exposure.ts:86`). A small colony on a large grid never has 1 % of the
cells. Then the white level falls onto the faint trail that the diffusion spreads around the plasm, and the gain
rises from 0.8 to 1,035 within 110 steps. Every faint cell is then drawn as thick gel. The model below holds fine
arms the whole time. With a white level that is never below the trail of the plasm, the same run shows fine arms
from step 60 on (`research/front/out/owner/flop-app.png` against `research/front/out/owner/flop-fix.png`). The change is small and touches the picture alone.

**2. The same bug hides the tubes of every small colony, and with the rules a colony stays small for long.** In Food
ring with the rules on, the app draws an even blob two to three times the size of the colony for 4,000 steps; with
the drift 0 for more than 16,000 steps. So a part of "an even sheet without tubes" is the picture. The other part is
the model, and three causes make it: (a) fresh ground starts with the credit, so every new piece of sheet counts as
a fed tube for about 1,000 steps and then starves; (b) when the colony covers a new food, the flux between foods
lifts D on the whole sheet, and the count of agents doubles within 1,000 steps and falls again; (c) every part of the
rim advances at the same speed. Two probes with a credit of 0.02 and 0.01 gave veins that fork from the core toward
the rim, a thin sheet and a lobed rim (`research/front/out/owner/ring-crown-fresh002-fix.png`, `research/front/out/owner/ring-crown-fresh001-fix.png`). They are single
runs with a patched constant: a lead, not a result. This is part E2o of ADR 0085, and it is the next research step.

**3. Three limits stop the growth, and none of them is food.** Jones' rule holds a density, not a mass: when the
mesh covers the dish, births end. The flow then starves every tube without flux and closes its ground for 160,000
steps: on a grid of 1536 × 864 cells the owner's link peaks at 35,498 agents at step 2,501 and falls to 3,353 at step
14,001, with 92 % of the dish closed for births (`research/front/out/owner/link-16x9-fix.png`). With the rules, births come from a fixed
budget of 2 · flow strength / 20 per step: 0.2 births per step at a flow strength of 2, whatever the colony covers.
The plan ties the budget to the covered food and to the size of the colony, in the prototype first.

**4. The texts of ChatGPT name real gaps and propose the wrong remedy.** True: the flux of the model moves no mass;
the front does not follow what the flow brings; the plasm has no thickness; nothing oscillates. Not supported: that
a separate physiological engine closes these gaps. Its reference models describe droplets below 1 mm with a fixed
edge (from memory), not networks of centimetres; one sign is wrong for Physarum; two of its equations are, in its own
words, not validated; and arms, tubes and the crown come last in its order. A rough cost is 7 million helper tokens
before the first picture of arms. The plan takes five small steps from it instead (section 4.3).

**The order.** Phase 0: fix the auto levels, and survey what stops the growth in each scene (about 250,000 tokens,
about 1 hour of GPU). The owner then looks again. Phase 1: three isolating studies in the prototype, side by side:
the credit, the boom at a new food, growth that follows the food (about 650,000 tokens). Pictures go to the owner
first. Phase 2: the rules that passed go into the engine (about 850,000 tokens). Phase 3, only if the rim is still
smooth: a rim that advances where the flux arrives (about 600,000 tokens). Section 5 has the gates.

**The owner's open decisions** (section 6): the physiological engine (the plan says: not now); growth that goes on
only with the rules (the scenes of papers keep their pruning); the budget of phases 1 and 2.

## 1. Observation 1: the flop

### 1.1 The mechanism

- `levels()` in `src/engine/exposure.ts:81` takes the white level as the trail that 1 % of **all cells of the grid**
  exceed (`:86`, `WHITE` at `:6`). The gain is 1.3 / (white − black) (`:91`).
- The histogram counts every cell with a trail above 0 (`src/engine/shaders/histogram.wgsl:22`). The trail is a
  float32 field (`src/engine/sim.ts:370`), and the 3 × 3 mean spreads it by one cell per step. So the cells with
  some trail grow by a ring in every step, far beyond the plasm.
- While fewer than 1 % of the cells hold any trail, `levels()` gives nothing (`:87`), and the frame draws with a
  fixed gain of 0.4 / equilibrium (`src/engine/webgpu.ts:168`, `:173`): 0.8 in the owner's link.
- From then on the white level lies in the faint trail around the plasm. The lowest bin of the histogram stands for
  2^-9.75, so the gain goes up to 1.3 / 2^-9.75 = 1,119. Where more than 35 % of the cells hold some trail, the black
  level is 0.8 of the white level (`:90`), and the gain goes up to 5,600.
- The frames take a quarter of each measurement, every sixth frame (`:146`, `:148`). So the jump takes less than a
  second on screen.

### 2.2 The mechanisms in the code

**B. The credit of fresh ground.** `FLOW.fresh` is 0.2 (`src/engine/flow.ts:61`). It is the memory after a reset
(`src/engine/sim.ts:970`) and the cap of ground without a tube (`src/engine/shaders/flow.wgsl:419`). A birth needs
D ≥ 0.01 on the cell of the parent and on the cell of the child (`FLOW.half`, `flow.ts:66`;
`src/engine/shaders/growth.wgsl:50`), and so does a paid birth (`docs/model.md`, 9.1). With the rules a tube adapts
from its first step (`fed()`, `flow.wgsl:162`): D moves toward f(Q) by 0.003 per step (`flow.wgsl:415`). In a sheet
the flux of a cell is small: a rim sink of the Crown of veins takes 0.030 (ADR 0087), and f(0.03) is 0.0018. So a
new cell of the sheet falls from 0.2 to 0.01 in 998 steps (`docs/model.md`, 8.6). For these steps Jones' rule keeps
it full of agents ("Jones' births refill the ground behind the front", `research/front/the-crown-at-the-head.md`).
Then it starves, and 1 to 2 % of its agents die per step (`src/engine/shaders/agents.wgsl:276`). A sheet on credit
shows no vein: ADR 0085 finds the first veins 22 to 23 cells behind the rim. It also conducts like a tube along the
rim, which is the coordinator's guess in ADR 0085 (reason 1) for the smooth rim.

**C. The flux between foods floods a sheet.** With two covered foods the sites take roles, and each gives or takes
the flow strength (`docs/model.md`, 8.2). The levers of the Crown of veins have a flow strength of 8; the scene has
one food, so its research never met this. In `ring-crown` the cells with D < 0.01 fall from 18,705 at step 9,001 to

two make the doubling; no run tells which. The boom stays with a credit of 0.01 (7,265, 12,285, 8,940 agents at
steps 9,001, 10,001, 11,001) and is weak with 0.02 (6,877, 7,534, 8,095 at steps 7,001, 8,001, 9,001): one seed
each, so the credit does not settle it.

**D. Every part of the rim advances at the same speed.** A cell joins the territory with the chance
1 − (1 − 0.004)^n, with n the agents among its 8 neighbours (`src/engine/territory.ts:16`, `:36`; `docs/model.md`,
6.10). n is at most 8, and one agent per cell caps the plasm. All sinks take equal shares by their weights (6.11,
8.7), and the drift brings plasm to every part of the rim. So no part at the head of a vein runs ahead. ADR 0085
says the same: lobes must come from the veins.

**E. Few veins with the default levers.** A vein lives where f(Q) ≥ 0.01, so where Q ≥ 0.077 per cell. With the
default levers all sinks take 4 together (2 · 1 · 2), with the levers of the Crown of veins 32 (2 · 2 · 8). So
`ring-territory` keeps 3 veins.

**F. The drift 0 gives no organism with arms.** The help of Drift says that 0 gives a tree
(`src/model/params.ts:395`). In the engine the colony waits 8,000 steps and then grows one arm. The prototype's
tree covered a third of a dish of 160 cells in 20,000 steps (`research/front/README.md`, step 9).

### 2.3 What the organism does

Sources: `docs/knowledge-base/slime-mazes-and-the-front.md`, "The front", and `docs/fidelity.md`, rows 6, 17, 19 and 24.

- A front is a thin sheet without visible structure. Veins appear in it, and the sheet between them thins away (Le
  Verge-Serandour and Alim 2023, Sec. 2.2, 4.1 and 5.1).
- A spot gives its mass to the veins with a time scale of 195 s, about 100 steps. The main vein shows about 50 min
  after the front, 1,500 steps (Schenz 2019, p. 52 and p. 30). In the model the sheet lives 1,000 steps on credit,
  and then its agents die: its mass does not go into the veins.
- Just behind the front many veins lie side by side. Farther back they join to a few and to one main vein (Schenz
  2019, Sec. 3.1.4). This is a tree that forks toward the rim: the owner's lightning.
- A front splits in two, and one of the two stops (Kuroda et al. 2015, p. 3736). Above about 1 mm a plasmodium has
  several fronts.
- On a rich medium the plasmodium grows as a mesh with a sheet at the front. Under starvation or with repellents it
  grows as trees of branches (Takamatsu et al. 2009). On poor ground it migrates fast and keeps main branches (the
  review, Sec. 4.2.1). So a round sheet and arms are both the organism. The owner asks for the arms.
- The fan swells and shrinks with the contractions, with a period of 1 to 2 min, 30 to 60 steps (row 17). Speed and
  shape also swing with 20 ± 10 min (Kuroda et al. 2015). The model has neither. Its boom of 1,000 to 2,000 steps
  copies none of them.

### 3.2 What the organism does

A plasmodium gains mass only from its food. Fed plasmodia are compact and stay; on poor ground they migrate, sparse
and wide (Dussutour et al. 2010, as `docs/knowledge-base/slime-mazes-and-the-front.md` cites it, "Fed and hungry
plasmodia"). Almost all of its plasm lies near the front (Kuroda et al. 2015, p. 3732), and a tube with more flow is
thicker (`docs/fidelity.md`, row 3). The docs hold no rate of growth of the mass: a helper must find one with its
source before a rule takes a number.

### 4.2 What a separate physiological engine would cost and give

**The scale decides.** From memory, the active poroelastic models (Radszuweit et al. 2013 and 2014) describe droplets
of protoplasm well below 1 mm with a fixed edge, and a later model of the same group moves a microplasmodium of that
size (Kulawiak et al. 2019). A cell of the app is 0.54 mm on the Tokyo scene (`docs/model.md`, 3). So the patterns of
those models are as large as one or two cells of the app. At the scale that the app shows, a plasmodium is a network
of tubes with a wave of contraction along it (Alim et al. 2013, in `TODO.md`, Later, not read yet) and a front that
takes the flow (Schenz 2019 and de Almeida 2021, both in the docs). The app is on this way already: the flow in the
tubes and the rules of the growth front.

**The cost**, an estimate from the costs of this repository, not a measurement. The crown research took nine parts
on a CPU prototype that existed, 2.33 million tokens, and it made no crown (ADR 0085). The physiological engine
needs a CPU reference in double precision for at least six coupled parts. Experiment A: about 500,000 tokens.
Experiment B: about 750,000. Experiment C is open research of at least the size of the crown research, 2.3 million,
with no promise. Then the engine: about eight new passes on two backends with references and tests, about 2.5
million. Calibration against the organism and scenes: about 1 million. Together about 7 million tokens and tens of
hours of GPU before the owner sees arms.

**The gain against the aims of the owner.**

- The model behaves like the organism: the engine could close row 17 (the pulse) and give the plasm a thickness.
  The rows that the app matches today (1, 2, 5 and 23) would have to be won again.
- Two models: it is a third one, so ADR 0022 needs a new decision.
- The three observations: the first is a bug of the picture. The third needs a budget of mass, which is a small
  rule. The second needs a front that follows its supply. The engine reaches none of them sooner.

**If no way reaches the aim.** No run of this project shows arms with a crown that moves on. The probes of section
2.2 show lobes and forking veins, in one seed each. If the studies of phases 1 and 3 fail too, their measures will
say so in numbers.

2. **Mass comes from the food and goes to the front** (claims 4 and 6): section 3.3, option 2. Phase 1.

4. **A vein holds plasm by its flux** (claim 14): section 3.3, option 3. Later.

A cheap step, if the owner wants a firmer base for this choice: one reading task of about 150,000 tokens. A helper
